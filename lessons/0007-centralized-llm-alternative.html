Lesson 7 — agentic_uav

A centralized, global-info LLM layer

The contrast that sharpens "decentralized" — re-introducing the oracle on purpose, as an upper bound.

Mission link. Every claim about decentralization is stronger when you can name what you gave up and why. A centralized global-info LLM is the natural ceiling; defining it explicitly lets you say exactly what the decentralized agentic method is trying to approach — and what it protects that the central one can't.

A. The idea, stated plainly

Imagine a single LLM "swarm commander." Every tick it ingests the full global state: every UAV's position, health, and energy; the true coverage of every sector; the complete list of urgent sectors; and the full communication graph. It reasons once, then emits one joint assignment for the entire swarm — a target and role for each UAV. One brain, total information, central command. No belief maps, no negotiation, no range limits: the commander simply sees everything and tells each drone where to go.

This is the obvious "smart" design, and a reviewer will ask why you didn't build it. The honest, useful answer is the whole point of this lesson.

B. It does not exist — read this first

Not in the codebase. There is no CentralLLMMethod in this repo today. This entire lesson describes a proposed contrast / extension, not something implemented. Treat every sketch below as a design on paper. By construction it would fit the existing SwarmMethod seam (it's still just a method: initialize_mission / decide_tick / handle_event returning a list[Action]) — but it would deliberately violate the two invariants: observation symmetry and decentralization. So it must never be mixed into the fair baseline comparison. It is reported on its own as a clearly-labelled centralized upper-bound reference.

If you take one thing from this page: the centralized layer is a yardstick, not a contender in the paired sweep. Say "proposed" and "upper-bound reference" out loud whenever you mention it, so no listener ever thinks it already runs.

C. This is exactly the oracle ADR-0001 removed

The centralized commander is, almost line for line, the global oracle that ADR-0001 deleted: the old global urgent_cells list plus the full world.sectors scans that let every method see all dynamic state at once.

Recall why the ADR removed it (ADR-0001, Context): that oracle was baked invisibly into every method, so "any 'agentic' advantage could just be the oracle, not the reasoning." It made the agentic-vs-baseline comparison invalid — the headline claim was unfalsifiable. The leak had to go for all methods at once.

The crisp distinction. The sin ADR-0001 banned was a hidden, unfair oracle smuggled into every baseline. It did not ban the concept of a centralized planner studied on its own. Re-introducing the oracle on purpose, as one explicit, separately-reported condition that you label loudly, is legitimate science. The difference is everything: smuggled in, it corrupts the comparison; declared and isolated, it becomes a measuring stick.

D. How it would plug in (sketch, not in repo)

Because it satisfies the SwarmMethod contract from L2, wiring it would look familiar — only the information it reads changes. The flow it would follow:

  1. Gather global truth. Read every UAV's pose/health/energy, true per-sector coverage, all urgent sectors, full comms graph. a deliberately GLOBAL serialization — the opposite of serialize_observation
  2. Build one prompt. Serialize the whole world into a single commander prompt. proposed; no such builder exists in the repo
  3. Call the same adapter. Reuse L4's provider seam — an AgentDecider-style decide(). the one piece that genuinely reuses existing code
  4. Emit a joint plan. Return one coordinated Action per UAV; validate each against the world. still returns list[Action], so the harness is unchanged

A minimal shape — to show the seam fit, not an implementation:

# SKETCH — NOT IN REPO. Illustrative only; proposed future work.
class CentralLLMMethod:  # would satisfy the SwarmMethod protocol
    def decide_tick(self, observations, state, simulation):
        # IGNORES the symmetric per-UAV observations on purpose:
        global_state = serialize_global(simulation.world)  # the deliberate oracle
        raw = self.decider.decide(global_state)              # reuse L4's AgentDecider
        return [validate_action(a, simulation) for a in raw["assignments"]]
Re-stating the obvious so it can't be misread. The serialize_global above is imaginary and intentionally the opposite of the real serialize_observation (L4), which emits only local keys. There is no CentralLLMMethod, no serialize_global, and no commander prompt in the codebase. This is a design sketch.

E. The trade-off — centralized vs decentralized

This is the table to internalize. The left column buys optimality; the right column buys the properties a real disaster deployment actually needs. The decentralized advantages are what the thesis is genuinely defending.

DimensionCentralized global-info LLM proposedDecentralized agentic the thesis
InformationGlobal ground truth, every tickLocal belief + range-limited peer messages
Coordination qualityPotentially optimal joint planNegotiated, approximate (in-tick deconfliction)
Single point of failureYes — lose the commander, lose the swarmNo — degrades gracefully, UAVs keep acting
Comms dependenceNeeds reliable uplink of all state + downlink of commandsRange-limited, tolerant of loss / blackout
ScalabilityPrompt & compute grow with swarm × gridPer-UAV, local — scales with the swarm
Under dropout / blackoutCommander may be blind or severed; swarm stallsEach UAV keeps acting on its own local belief
Realism for real UAVsOften impractical to fieldThe motivating use case

Read the bottom four rows aloud when you present. A central commander is only as good as the link that feeds it and the link that carries its orders. Sever either — exactly what a disaster does — and the "optimal" plan never arrives. The decentralized swarm has no such single thread to cut.

F. Why this strengthens the defense

The contrast does three jobs for you.
  1. It gives an upper-bound reference. Your headline becomes quantitative: "the decentralized agent reaches X% of the centralized planner's recovery while tolerating comm loss the centralized one cannot." A ceiling makes "good" mean something.
  2. It makes the contribution legible. The point was never "LLMs are good." It is "an LLM can reason well from local information, decentralized" — and the ceiling is precisely what isolates that claim.
  3. It pre-empts the obvious reviewer. "Why not just centralize?" now has a principled, measured answer instead of a hand-wave: you considered it, defined it, and chose resilience.

Naming the thing you deliberately did not build is a stronger position than pretending it wasn't an option. The ceiling is your friend.

G. How you'd evaluate it fairly

You already have the machinery — the seeded scenarios and recovery metrics from L5. The evaluation would simply add a third tier:

  1. Run the same seeded scenarios and the same recovery metrics (recovery_slope, recovery_ticks_to_target) as L5 — no new ruler.
  2. Report three tiers, kept separate: heuristic baselines → decentralized agentic → centralized upper bound.
  3. Make the gap between decentralized agentic and the centralized bound the headline story — especially under dropout and comm_blackout.
  4. Test the centralized layer precisely in the comms-degraded scenarios, where centralization should suffer most — that exposes its fragility instead of flattering it.

The interesting number is not "the commander wins on a perfect link" (it should). It's "how close does decentralized agentic get without the oracle — and how far does the commander fall when the link drops?" That comparison is the thesis in one chart.

Defense drill — let's say it before we reveal it

Read the likely objection, say how we'd answer, then reveal the version we've been refining. Us pressure-testing our own argument before a reviewer does.

Attack 1: "If a central LLM with global info does better, why bother with decentralization?"

Because the central planner needs an oracle and a reliable uplink/command channel that real disaster deployments lack. Under dropout or comm_blackout it degrades or fails outright, while decentralized agents keep acting on local belief. The thesis values resilience and graceful degradation, not just peak coverage on a perfect link.

Attack 2: "Isn't adding a centralized method just bringing back the oracle ADR-0001 banned?"

No. The ban was on a hidden oracle baked into every method, which made the comparison unfair and the claim unfalsifiable. An explicit, separately-reported centralized reference is legitimate science — it's labelled and isolated, not smuggled into the baselines. The sin was the concealment, not the concept.

Attack 3: "Could the centralized layer reuse your existing code?"

Yes for the seam (the SwarmMethod contract plus the AgentDecider adapter from L4) and for metrics/sweeps (L5). No for observation-building: it would need a deliberately global serialization — the exact opposite of the symmetric per-UAV serialize_observation. That one piece is where it breaks the invariant on purpose.
