B. Not yet proven — the honest core gap

There is no measured advantage today. The default decider is MockAgentDecider, which deterministically mirrors greedy (llm_agent.py:180–184 docstring; test_mirrors_greedy_choosing_uncovered_when_no_urgent at test_llm_agent.py:153). So today agentic ≈ greedy. The headline empirical claim — "beats baselines on recovery after disruption" — is currently untested with a real reasoner. State this plainly: the defense today rests on architecture + fairness, not on a measured win.

This is the single sentence to rehearse until it's reflexive: "The thesis is currently unevaluated, not refuted — the default decider is a mock that mirrors greedy by design to test the plumbing." A reviewer who hears you say it first reads it as rigour, not weakness. A reviewer who discovers it themselves reads it as overclaiming.

C. The complexity-inversion footnote

An earlier audit is now stale here — be precise about why. report.md (§3) said agentic was the simplest method (~15 lines, "simpler than all three baselines"). That is no longer true: in current policy.py, AgenticMethod (212–393) is the largest, most structured method — ~180 lines, with a replan/execute split, in-tick deconfliction, and plan persistence. But say exactly what that buys: it is orchestration, not yet smarter per-UAV reasoning. The reasoning is delegated to the mocked decider. Don't let the line count tempt you into overclaiming intelligence — the scaffolding grew; the reasoner did not.

E. The path to a genuine result

Highest-leverage step first. The structural work is done; what remains is mostly config and analysis, not new architecture.

  1. Wire a real vendor behind the adapter. Set AGENTIC_UAV_DECIDER=bedrock or qgenie. The seam already exists (L4) — this is config, not new architecture. THIS IS THE UNLOCK.
  2. Run the paired seeded sweep. agentic vs the 4 baselines on identical scenarios. The L5 command — same fairness machinery, now with a real reasoner.
  3. Add significance testing + recovery curves to aggregation. Closes the L5 mean+SEM gap so "higher" can become "significantly higher."
  4. Report recovery AND overhead. recovery_slope / ticks_to_target plus messages / role switches / replan count vs baselines. The claim is supported only if agentic is faster post-disruption while staying competitive on overhead.

Step 1 is the unlock. Everything structural — the seam, the validator, the fallback, the paired sweep, the recovery metrics — is already in place. The experiment is not blocked on building anything; it is blocked on flipping an env var and running the sweep.

F. What I can claim today vs. after step 1

Today (defensible now)

"We built a fair, decentralized, swappable harness with a real LLM seam and recovery instrumentation. The architecture enforces observation symmetry and decentralization by construction — single selection site, removed global oracle with a regression test, belief kept off the world model."

After wiring a vendor + sweep

"…and the LLM agent empirically recovers faster than the baselines under disruption while staying competitive on overhead." This is the sentence the experiment is for — do not say it before the sweep runs.

Defense drill — our hardest questions, answered honestly

Read the objection, say how we'd answer, then reveal the version we've been refining. These are the ones a sharp reviewer will ask — let's own them before they do.

Attack 1: "Your results show agentic = greedy. Doesn't that refute your thesis?"

No result has been run with a real reasoner. The default is a mock that mirrors greedy by design — its job is to exercise the full serialize→validate→action pipeline, not to demonstrate a win. The thesis is currently unevaluated, not refuted. Wiring a vendor behind the existing adapter seam is the only missing step.

Attack 2: "Why should I believe the harness is fair?"

Because fairness is structural and tested, not promised: a single method-selection site (build_method), a removed global oracle (urgent_cells) with a regression test asserting its absence, belief kept off the world model, and identical observations emitted to all five methods. They differ in reasoning, never in what they can see. (L2 / L3.)

Attack 3: "Isn't a 180-line method that delegates to a mock just dressed-up greedy?"

The 180 lines are real orchestration a baseline lacks: event-triggered replan, in-tick deconfliction, and plan persistence. But the per-UAV reasoning quality depends on the decider, which is currently mocked. Honest answer: the scaffolding is the contribution-in-waiting; the reasoning is the experiment not yet run. I won't claim the orchestration is the intelligence.
