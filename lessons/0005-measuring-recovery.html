Lesson 5 — agentic_uav

Measuring recovery after disruption

How "beats the baselines on recovery" becomes an actual, paired, seeded number.

Mission link. Your thesis isn't "the LLM is smart" — it's a measurable claim about behaviour after a disruption. This lesson is the operational definition you must be able to defend cold: what counts as recovery, what gets logged, and what the sweep actually compares. If you can't state these crisply, a reviewer will define them for you — unfavourably.

A. The disruptions you inject

Recovery is only meaningful relative to a shock. The shocks are scheduled events fired at a fixed tick by EventInjector.apply (simulation.py:87–115). Five types:

EventEffectWhere
dropoutDeactivates a UAV: active=False, health="dropped". Payload {"uav_id": …}.103–107
block_sectorMarks a sector blocked=True.100–102
urgent_sectorSets a sector's priority="urgent".108–110
visibility_degradeDrops a sector's visibility (default 0.5).111–114
comm_blackoutAdds a BlackoutZone to the network (handled in step()).~178

The headline metric keys off dropout: it removes a UAV mid-mission and forces the remaining swarm to keep covering ground with less capacity. That's the moment the thesis is about.

B. What "recovery" actually means here

Read this before a reviewer does. Coverage in this simulator is monotonic — a dropout does not un-cover already-covered sectors. So recovery_slope does NOT measure "regaining lost coverage." Quoting the protocol verbatim (EXPERIMENTS.md:19–26): "recovery_slope does not measure 'regaining lost coverage.' It measures continued progress under reduced swarm capacity." Say it that way every time. "Recovery" = sustained forward progress despite the loss, not clawing back something that decreased.

This is the single most important caveat in the whole evaluation. If you let a listener assume coverage drops and is "recovered," every number you quote is misread. State the monotonicity first, then the metric.

C. The two headline recovery metrics

Both live in experiments.py and key off the dropout_tick:

recovery_slope()
Coverage gain per tick over a 20%-of-mission window after the dropout (experiments.py:306–330). Higher = the survivors kept making progress through the shock. Its own docstring repeats the monotonicity caveat.
ticks_to_reach_coverage() → recovery_ticks_to_target
Ticks elapsed after the dropout until coverage first re-reaches the success threshold (default 0.9) (experiments.py:333–353). Returns None if the target is never hit. This is the "how fast did the swarm get back to target" number.

The claim rests on these two. recovery_slope is the rate; recovery_ticks_to_target is the time. One can move without the other, which is why both are reported.

D. Overhead metrics — so a "win" isn't just thrashing

run_trial returns ~25 metrics per trial (experiments.py:154–194). Alongside the two recovery numbers it logs the cost of the activity: replan_count, plan_change_count, role_switches, target_changes, messages_sent, urgent_response_time, and completion_tick.

The "beats" definition has two halves (EXPERIMENTS.md:49–61). Against the best baseline on the same pinned scenario, agentic must (1) sustain faster post-disruption progress and/or re-reach target sooner — while staying competitive on overhead (messages, role switches, replan count). That clause is what makes it a real result. A recovery win bought with a blow-up in communication or constant role-thrashing is explicitly not a clean win.

Emphasise the "while staying competitive on overhead" clause when you present. It pre-empts the obvious rebuttal — "couldn't it just do more?" — by showing the activity is already on the table.

E. The metrics plumbing

MetricsLogger (simulation.py:23–84) records a per-tick log plus two persistent traces:

  • Per-tick log (57–69): coverage_ratio, active_uavs, messages_sent, plus per-UAV uav_cells / uav_roles / uav_targets, and urgent_targets. This is what the recovery metrics replay over.
  • self.replans & self.plan_changes (init 25–30): the disruption-response traces.
# simulation.py:32 — agentic calls this when it re-plans (the L4 event-triggered hook)
def log_replan(self, tick: int, reason: str, uav_ids: list[str]) -> None:
    self.replans.append(
        {"tick": tick, "reason": reason, "uav_ids": sorted(uav_ids)}
    )

log_plan_change(tick, uav_id, old/new target, old/new role) (37–55) is the per-tick, per-UAV change log, called from resolve_actions (~236). summary() (71–84) returns ticks_run, coverage_ratio, messages_sent, urgent_targets, is_solved, termination_reason. Tie this back to L4: the event-triggered re-plan is what populates self.replans, so the trace and the metric come from the same hook.

F. The paired, seeded sweep

The fairness lives in one line: METHODS = ("static","rules","task_consideration","greedy","agentic") (experiments.py:17). All five run on the same seeded scenarios — the seed pins identical dropouts and urgent events for every method. That's the "paired" comparison, the same fairness machinery you saw in L2/L3.

ExperimentSuiteConfig defaults (experiments.py:22–36): swarm_sizes=(5,10,15), dropout_fractions=(0,0.1,0.25,0.4), seed_count=20, ticks=500. Two families (69–74): survey_dropout and disaster_urgent_dropout.

  1. Configure sweep ExperimentSuiteConfig: sizes × dropout fractions × seeds × methods
  2. Run paired seeded trials same seed → same world → all 5 methods (experiments.py:412–469)
  3. Per-tick metrics + replan / plan-change traces MetricsLogger (simulation.py:23–84)
  4. Aggregate mean + SEM across seeds aggregate_trials, grouped 5-tuple (experiments.py:197–240)
  5. Write CSV / JSON / plots trials & aggregates + dropout / success / timeline plots (243–290)

aggregate_trials (197–240) groups by (family, method, mission_type, swarm_size, dropout_fraction) and computes mean() and sem() across seeds. Outputs (write_experiment_outputs 243–258): trials.csv/json, aggregates.csv/json, plus plots (261–290) — dropout-vs-final-coverage, dropout-vs-success-rate, and a representative coverage(tick) timeline.

Aggregation gap. The aggregate computes only mean and SEM — there are no confidence intervals and no significance tests (sem() at experiments.py:565 is the whole of it). You can report "agentic's mean recovery slope is higher, ± SEM," but you cannot yet say "significantly higher." Flag this yourself before anyone asks.

G. Running it — the CLI

The experiment subcommand (main.py:297–314) exposes: --output-dir (default runs/experiments), --seed-count (20), --base-seed (0), --swarm-sizes ("5,10,15"), --dropout-fractions ("0,0.1,0.25,0.4"), --ticks (500), --grid-size (25), --success-threshold (0.9), --family (all | survey_dropout | disaster_urgent_dropout), --no-plots.

The fast smoke command (EXPERIMENTS.md:82–87):

uv run main.py experiment --family disaster_urgent_dropout \
  --seed-count 1 --swarm-sizes 6 --ticks 120 --grid-size 12 \
  --no-plots --output-dir runs/smoke
Smoke ≠ result. The default MockAgentDecider mirrors greedy (EXPERIMENTS.md:71–78), so under the mock, agentic behaves like greedy. A smoke run verifies plumbing — the event-triggered re-plan fires, negotiation resolves, traces and the new recovery columns are written — not an agentic advantage. Do not read mock numbers as a finding (ties to L4; the real-LLM result is L6's subject).

H. Honest gaps in the measurement

Be precise and fair: these are refinements, not fatal flaws. The headline metrics exist, and they're paired and seeded. But a recovery claim would ideally also have things the code does not compute:

WantedWhat the code does instead
Time-to-first-reassignment after dropoutPlan changes are counted, never timed to the first change.
A full recovery curve with confidence bandsOnly a single slope + a hit-time.
Replan-trigger latencylog_replan stores tick + reason + uavs, not the triggering event's tick.
Coordination / negotiation traces over timeNot captured as a timeseries.
Statistical significance / effect sizeOnly mean + SEM (no tests).

Defense drill — let's rehearse aloud

Read the likely objection, say how we'd answer, then reveal the version we've been refining. Us pressure-testing our own argument before a reviewer does.

Attack 1: "If coverage never decreases, what does 'recovery' even mean?"

Coverage is monotonic, so recovery is continued progress under reduced swarm capacity, not regaining lost ground (EXPERIMENTS.md:19–26). I measure it two ways: recovery_slope is the post-dropout coverage gain rate over a 20% window, and recovery_ticks_to_target is how many ticks after the dropout the swarm takes to re-reach 0.9 coverage.

Attack 2: "Couldn't agentic 'win' just by spamming messages or switching roles constantly?"

No — the "beats" definition (EXPERIMENTS.md:49–61) requires competitive overhead. messages_sent, role_switches, and replan_count are all logged and reported per trial, so thrashing shows up directly in the numbers. A recovery win bought with a comms blow-up is explicitly not counted as a clean win.

Attack 3: "Is the comparison even fair across methods?"

Yes — same seeded scenarios, same injected events, all five methods, fully paired (the seed pins identical dropouts and urgent events). Results aggregate over seed_count=20 seeds with mean + SEM. The honest caveat: there's no significance test yet, only mean ± SEM — a known gap, not a fairness flaw.
